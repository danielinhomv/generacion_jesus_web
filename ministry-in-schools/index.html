History & Purpose

Decades of Relationships. One Clear Purpose.

The roots of this ministry grow out of a calling that began in the jungle — serving children whose lives had been shattered by conflict. The schools program grew from that same conviction: reach children before crisis reaches them, and do it through relationships that last.

The curriculum and chaplaincy are the means. Evangelism, discipleship, and multiplication are the purpose.

  1. 1989

    The calling begins in the Honduran jungle.

    While serving people displaced by the Sandinista conflict near the Honduras–Nicaragua border, Rocky Malloy encountered children whose lives had been devastated by war. A twelve-year-old boy repeatedly asking for milk for his orphaned baby sister — and the sight of that little girl in a dirty white dress — awakened a lifelong calling: protect children, introduce them to Jesus Christ, and help them build their lives on His Word.

  2. 1990

    Shield of Faith Ministries takes shape in Bolivia.

    The calling took form through Shield of Faith Ministries in Santa Cruz, Bolivia. Evangelism, Bible teaching, and ministry to children and families established the foundation for the international work that followed.

  3. 2005

    Reaching children before the crisis.

    Ministry to vulnerable children in Bolivia reinforced Rocky and Joske's conviction that intervention must begin before exploitation, neglect, and harmful circumstances take hold. Schools offered a way to reach children consistently while building relationships with educators, parents, churches, and community leaders.

  4. 2014

    A character curriculum opens school doors.

    A character-development curriculum provided a practical means to enter schools and share biblical principles. Those educational relationships created opportunities for Gospel ministry among students, teachers, and families. The program served a larger purpose: introducing people to Jesus Christ and helping them grow as disciples.

  5. 2019

    Chaplaincy deepens daily ministry and drives international expansion.

    Training school chaplains and spiritual advisors — many already employed in schools — provided greater ongoing access and a more financially efficient approach to evangelism and discipleship. These leaders remained in their communities, built trust, prayed with people, taught Scripture, and ministered throughout the entire school year.

    International expansion began in Paraguay and later extended across Spanish-speaking countries. A deliberately decentralized structure equipped local participants to carry the mission inside their own schools and communities.

  6. 2026

    Jesus Generation renews its U.S. connection.

    Jesus Generation — the U.S. public ministry name of Forming Lives — restores its public presence, supporting the ongoing international work carried out through Formando Vidas. Current reports describe approximately 53,000 school chaplains and spiritual advisors serving in 44,000 schools across 24 nations and territories. Exact figures and report definitions are being finalized for publication.

Who Chaplains Are

Already in the School. Already Trusted.

Approximately 87 percent of certified participants are already school employees serving in a variety of roles when they enter the program. They are not paid staff of Jesus Generation or Formando Vidas — they are teachers, counselors, and administrators who bring the Gospel with them into work they already do.

Formando Vidas provides recruitment, training, certification, continuing education, and support resources. Live webinars twice a month offer ongoing training. A support line helps participants apply training in real school situations, Bible studies, and community challenges.

Local by design

Chaplains live in the communities they serve. They know the families, the pressures students face, and the people who need the Gospel most.

Trained & certified

Every participant completes Formando Vidas training and certification before serving. Continuing education keeps them equipped throughout the year.

Grounded in Scripture

Teaching the Bible is central to everything. Chaplains lead Bible studies, share the Gospel, disciple students, and connect families to local churches.

Present all year

Unlike a visiting team that stays a week, chaplains are there every day — available for prayer, conversation, and care whenever a student needs them.

Ministry in Practice

Prayer. The Gospel. The Word. Discipleship.

These are not programs. They happen inside the ongoing relationships that chaplains hold with their school communities — day after day, year after year.

Prayer & pastoral care

Chaplains pray with students, teachers, and families — in corridors, classrooms, and community spaces. They listen when no one else does.

Gospel conversations

Sharing who Jesus is, what He did, and why it matters — through natural, honest conversations built on trust that has been earned over time.

Bible studies

Among reported activities are Bible studies held during regular school hours.

Discipleship & church connection

Helping new believers grow in their faith and connecting them with local churches is a core part of the mission. These relationships and resources are continually being strengthened.

Why This Looks Different

International Schools Are Not U.S. Public Schools.

Americans sometimes ask why chaplains can serve inside government schools in Latin America. The answer is that the legal, cultural, and institutional context is genuinely different.

Relationships, permissions, and applicable requirements vary by country and locality. Public schools are a core part of the international work.

The ministry does not claim that all participating countries share a single constitutional or religious framework, or that all religious activities are permitted in all schools. What it has built is decades of trusted relationships — and those relationships open doors that rules alone cannot.

01

Relationships before programs

Access was not granted overnight. It grew over decades through consistent service, demonstrated character, and genuine care for students and educators.

02

Local leaders carry the mission

Nationals — not visiting foreigners — are the primary ministers. That matters for trust, for sustainability, and for how communities receive the Gospel.

03

Decentralized by design

The structure was deliberately built so that local participants carry the mission inside their own schools and communities — without depending on a central office to function.

Child Protection

Protecting Children Is Central to This Ministry.

A chaplain's ongoing presence creates something a one-time visit cannot: the trust that lets a child speak, and the awareness to recognize when something is wrong.

Recognizing danger

Chaplains are trained to notice signs of manipulation, abuse, and exploitation — and to help children understand their own dignity and worth.

Listening & responding

When a child speaks up, a chaplain is there to listen, support, and facilitate appropriate referrals. Criminal investigation belongs to legal authorities.

Community-wide awareness

Chaplains' relationships with educators and families strengthen the whole community's ability to detect and respond when a child is at risk.

Why we share carefully

Laws protecting minors, and threats from criminal organizations, limit what can be published. Anonymized, aggregate reporting protects the people involved. The absence of names and photos reflects that protection, not a lack of work.

According to international operations reports, chaplains have helped dismantle hundreds of sexual trafficking networks. This work can expose them to significant personal risk. Operational security and protection of children, families, and chaplains require that specific details not be shared publicly.

Visiting Mission Teams

You Complement. They Continue.

Visiting teams don't replace local leaders — they encourage them. A team comes alongside established chaplains and community leaders to share testimonies, present the Gospel, pray with people, encourage educators and chaplains, and support outreach in schools, churches, and communities.

When the team flies home, the ministry continues. The chaplains are still there tomorrow morning.
